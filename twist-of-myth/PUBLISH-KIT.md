# Twist of Myth: publish kit

Scripts for episodes 1 to 3 are in `docs/storybook-channel-launch-kit.md`, section 5. They are
original retellings (about 330 to 380 words each, 2.5 to 3 minutes at the storybook pace).
Thumbnails are in `channel-assets/thumbnails/`. The text version is the fallback; once an
episode is generated, put the best painted frame on the right and keep the text layout.

Rules that never change:
- Title formula: "The [thing] that [surprising outcome]" or "Why [name] [did the unexpected]". Under 60 characters.
- Description: hook line, two-sentence summary without the twist, source, AI disclosure.
- Tick "Altered or synthetic content". Made for kids: No (the stories involve death, curses and demons, and a kids flag kills comments and most ad revenue).
- Every episode is also cut into one vertical Short: the twist, 45 to 60 seconds.
- Add a Hindi audio track after publishing.

## Episode 1 · Trishanku

Thumbnail: `channel-assets/thumbnails/twist-of-myth-01-trishanku.png`
Thumbnail text: STUCK BETWEEN HEAVEN AND EARTH / The king still hanging upside down

**Title (A is the default)**
```
A: The King Who Is Still Hanging Upside Down in the Sky
B: Why a King Is Stuck Between Heaven and Earth Forever
C: He Wanted Heaven Without Dying. The Gods Said No.
```

**Description**
```
Somewhere above you, right now, a king is hanging upside down. He has been there for a very long time.

Satyavrata of Ayodhya wanted to enter heaven alive, in his own body. Every sage refused him, until one sage agreed, for the wrong reasons. What happened next gave India a phrase it still uses for being stuck between two choices.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, sargas 57 to 60. This is an original retelling.
Narration and animation were produced with AI tools and checked by a person before publishing.

New tale twice a week. Old myths and legends, retold with a twist.

```

## Episode 2 · King Shibi

Thumbnail: `channel-assets/thumbnails/twist-of-myth-02-shibi.png`
Thumbnail text: A KING, A DOVE, AND A SCALE / What he put on the other side

**Title (A is the default)**
```
A: The King Who Climbed Onto the Scale to Save a Dove
B: A Dove Outweighed a King. Then He Understood the Trick.
C: Why King Shibi Cut His Own Flesh for a Bird
```

**Description**
```
A king once cut flesh from his own thigh to feed a bird. Then he cut more. Then he stopped, because he understood the trick.

A dove crashed into King Shibi's lap, and a hawk landed on his throne demanding its meal. The king asked for a scale. What the dove weighed, and what finally balanced it, is why this story has been told for over two thousand years.

Source: Mahabharata, Vana Parva (the Shibi upakhyana); also the Shibi Jataka. This is an original retelling.
Narration and animation were produced with AI tools and checked by a person before publishing.

New tale twice a week. Old myths and legends, retold with a twist.

```
A: The Demon Who Could Burn Anyone He Touched (For an Hour)
B: He Asked Shiva for One Power. Then Tried It on Shiva.
C: Why the God of Destruction Had to Run
```

**Description**
```
He had one power. Anyone he touched on the head turned to ash. Within an hour he had used it on himself.

Bhasmasura prayed for years and Shiva gave him exactly what he asked for. The demon's first idea was to test it on the god. What stopped him was not a weapon and not a bigger god.

Source: Bhagavata Purana, Skandha 10, chapter 88; retold across the Puranas and in Kathakali. This is an original retelling.
Narration and animation were produced with AI tools and checked by a person before publishing.
