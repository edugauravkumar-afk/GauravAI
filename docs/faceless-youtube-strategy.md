# Faceless YouTube Channel Plan (modelled on "easy, actually")

Research date: 3 October 2026. Direct access to youtube.com, vidIQ, Social Blade and
Favoree was blocked from this environment, so channel facts below come from search
snippets of those sites and may lag the live numbers by a few weeks.

have made breakdown videos such as "Why easy, actually is a YouTube GENIUS" and
"How This New Channel Broke YouTube in 2 Months".

## 2. Why it works (the parts you must copy)

1. **One title formula = a brand.** "X is easy, actually" is both the channel name and
   every title. A viewer who sees one video instantly understands the whole channel.
   The comma and lowercase are deliberate: casual, slightly contrarian, human.
2. **Contrarian promise on a painful topic.** Every topic is something the viewer has
   already failed at (weight, money, language, focus). "Easy, actually" directly
   contradicts their belief, which creates the click. The video then has to earn it.
3. **Few words, more pictures.** Scripts are short declarative sentences. Every sentence
   gets a drawing. No stock footage, no B-roll, no text walls. Retention stays high
   because something new appears on screen every 2-4 seconds.
4. **Evergreen topics with huge search and browse demand.** Nothing is tied to news.
   A 2024 video still gets recommended in 2026.
5. **Low volume, high quality.** About two videos a month. Each one is a finished
   essay, not a daily upload. The algorithm rewards the view-per-video ratio.
6. **Extreme visual consistency.** Thumbnails: white background, one stick figure, one
   object, three to five words, same font. The channel page looks like one product.
7. **No face, no ego.** The narrator never says "I". The content feels like a
   textbook written by a friend, which travels across countries and ages.

## 3. Pick your angle (do not clone the exact niche)

Cloning "self-improvement, stick figures, 'easy, actually'" exactly will get you
flagged as a copycat by viewers and ranked under the original by YouTube. Keep the
mechanics, change one of the three variables: topic lane, framing word, or visual signature.

Suggested lanes, ranked by fit for a faceless explainer in 2026:

| Lane | Why | Example titles |
|---|---|---|
| **Money & careers for people in their 20s** | Highest CPM, evergreen, endless pain points | "salary negotiation is simple, actually", "your first 1 crore is boring, actually" |
| **Tech and AI explained to normal people** | Massive demand, you already work with AI (this repo) | "AI agents are simple, actually", "coding with AI is cheating, actually" |
| **Health habits (sleep, focus, fitness)** | Proven by the original channel | "sleeping well is boring, actually" |
| **India-specific life skills** (UPI, taxes, loans, exams) | Underserved in English, huge audience | "filing ITR is easy, actually" |

Recommendation: start with **tech and AI explained to normal people** as the primary
lane, with money topics as a second pillar. It is the lane you can write with real
authority, and authority is what makes a contrarian title believable.

## 4. Channel name ideas

Checklist for a name:
- Works as a sentence frame you can reuse in every title.
- Lowercase, two or three words, easy to say.
- Handle is free on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and as a .com or .in (verify yourself; this environment could not reach YouTube).
- Does not contain "easy, actually" or "actually" as the hook word (that is theirs).

Shortlist (title frame shown in brackets):

1. **simpler than that** ("investing is simpler than that")  — closest in spirit, still distinct.
2. **not that hard** ("learning to code is not that hard")  — blunt, memorable, broad.
3. **turns out** ("turns out, saving money is boring")  — flexible, invites curiosity.
4. **the short version** ("AI agents, the short version")  — fits "few words, more pictures" promise.
5. **plainly** ("taxes, plainly")  — one word, premium feel, great for a logo.
6. **less complicated** ("negotiation is less complicated than you think").
7. **oh, that's it?** ("compound interest. oh, that's it?")  — playful, strong thumbnail text.
8. **in one drawing** ("inflation in one drawing")  — unique visual promise, hard to copy.

Top pick: **simpler than that** or **the short version**. Both give you a title
formula on day one, both are brandable, and neither leans on the original's wording.

Avoid: anything with "easy", "actually", "simplified", "explained", "101" or "hub".
These are saturated and generic.

## 5. Production system (faceless, one person)

### Tools

| Step | Free / cheap option | Paid upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Research & outline | Claude, Google Trends, YouTube search autocomplete | vidIQ or TubeBuddy for keyword volume |
| Script | Google Docs; 1,100-1,400 words for 8-10 min | — |
| Drawing | Excalidraw (free, hand-drawn look), Krita, or Procreate on iPad | Adobe Illustrator |
| Animation | DaVinci Resolve (free) or CapCut desktop with keyframes | After Effects |
| Voice | Your own voice through a USB mic (recommended) | ElevenLabs if you must stay silent; disclose it in upload settings |
| Music | YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay | Epidemic Sound |
| Thumbnails | Canva or Figma; same template every time | — |
| Captions | YouTube auto captions, then correct them | — |

Use your own voice if at all possible. Synthetic voice is allowed and monetisable,
but YouTube's 2025-2026 "inauthentic content" rules demonetise channels that feel
templated. A real voice plus hand-drawn frames is the cheapest insurance.

### Workflow per video (target: 8-10 hours)

1. **Topic** (30 min): pick a topic people have tried and failed at. Check YouTube
   search suggestions and competitor titles for demand.
2. **Thesis** (15 min): one sentence that contradicts the viewer's belief. If you cannot
   write it, drop the topic.
3. **Script** (2-3 hours): hook in the first 15 seconds that states the contradiction
   and promises the reveal. Then 3-5 sections, each a single idea with a single
   drawing. Short sentences. End with one action the viewer can take today. No intro,
   no "welcome back", no "like and subscribe" until the last 10 seconds.
4. **Storyboard** (1 hour): one drawing per sentence or two. 80-120 frames per video.
5. **Draw** (2-3 hours): black line stick figures, white background, one accent colour.
   Reuse a character library so it gets faster every video.
6. **Record** (30 min): read slowly, warm tone, two takes.
7. **Edit** (2 hours): cut every pause; a new frame every 2-4 seconds; light zooms only.
8. **Package** (30 min): thumbnail from template, title in the formula, description
   with 2-3 sentences and timestamps.

### Visual rules (write these down and never break them)

- White or off-white background, black line weight constant.
- One accent colour for the whole channel (pick one now; e.g. a warm orange).
- Same font everywhere (thumbnails, in-video text, end screen).
- Thumbnail = one figure + one object + three to five lowercase words.
- 16:9, 1920x1080 minimum.

## 6. 90-day launch plan

**Week 1-2: Foundation**
- Register name and handle on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, and the domain.
- Design the character, pick accent colour and font, make a thumbnail template.
- Write 10 titles in your formula. Keep the best 6.
- Channel banner: one line promise, e.g. "hard things, one drawing at a time".

**Week 3-6: First 4 videos**
- Publish one video per week. Do not publish until a video is better than the last.
- Also cut 3 vertical Shorts from each (one idea each, same drawings) to seed subscribers.
- Post the same Shorts on Instagram Reels and TikTok.

**Week 7-12: Learn and double down**
- Read retention graphs in YouTube Studio. Find where people leave; fix that in the next script.
- Keep the best performing topic cluster and make 2 more videos in it.
- Reply to every comment in the first 24 hours.
- Target by day 90: 8-10 long videos, 25-30 Shorts, first video over 10K views.

Monetisation needs 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months
(or 10M Shorts views in 90 days). With this format most channels reach that between
month 4 and month 8. Do not run ads, affiliates or sponsorship talk before then; it
hurts retention when you most need it.

## 7. Risks and how to avoid them

- **Copycat perception.** Change the hook word and the visual accent. Never reuse their
  exact phrasing or thumbnail layout.
- **Inauthentic-content demonetisation.** Original drawings, your voice, real scripts
  with a point of view. No AI stock slideshows.
- **Burnout from volume.** One good video a week beats three weak ones. The original
  channel reached 1M on roughly 24 videos.
- **Medical, financial and legal claims.** Add a one-line disclaimer in the description
  for health and money videos, and cite one source on screen when you state a number.
- **Music and image copyright.** Only use library music and your own drawings.
